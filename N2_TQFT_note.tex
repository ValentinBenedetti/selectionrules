\section{Rule: nodes are made of full superfields}

A superprimary and its superdescendants must enter an algebra together. The building blocks are therefore not the sectors $(l,m,s)$ of the bosonic subalgebra, but the NS superfields
\begin{equation}
[l,m]\equiv\big[(l,m,0)\oplus(l,m,2)\big]\otimes\overline{\big[(l,-m,0)\oplus(l,-m,2)\big]},\qquad l+m\ \text{even},\qquad [l,m]\sim[k-l,\,m+K],
\end{equation}
with $K=k+2$.
Each superfield contains all its components: the bottom component, the $G_{-1/2}$, $\bar G_{-1/2}$ and $G_{-1/2}\bar G_{-1/2}$ descendants, and the D-term. The fermionic mixed components are included, so the chiral algebra is the full $N=2$ algebra with $G^\pm$.

In the bosonic diagonal model of N2Gab (2.5) the left-only descendants $(l,m,2)\otimes\overline{(l,-m,0)}$ are absent. That is why the sector-by-sector analysis of the previous versions was not a supersymmetric one.

For odd $k$ every superfield has a unique representative with $l$ even, and then $m$ is even mod $2K$. The superfield fusion is $su(2)_k$ on $l$ and addition on $m$, so
\begin{equation}
\{\text{NS superfields}\}\ \simeq\ \so\times\Z_K,\qquad [l,m]\leftrightarrow(l\in\so,\ m/2\in\Z_K),\qquad d_{[l,m]}=d_l .
\end{equation}
There are $(k+1)(k+2)/2$ superfields. The Verlinde lines that commute with the $N=2$ algebra are labelled by the same set. The line $L_{[L,M]}$ acts on $[l,m]$ uniformly on all components, by $\frac{S_{Ll}}{S_{0l}}e^{i\pi Mm/K}$. Here:
\begin{itemize}
\item $L_{[L,0]}$ ($L$ even) are the $\so$ lines;
\item $L_{[0,2n]}$ is the phase symmetry $X\to\omega^nX$ (N2Gab \S5.4).
\end{itemize}
A node $T$, its commuting lines $F_T$ and the global index $\mu_T=(\sum_{F_T}d^2)^2$ are defined as in selection.pdf and paul.pdf, now with superfields.

\emph{What drops out.} In the sector-by-sector analysis we found extra lines, $L_{(0,0,2)}$ and $L_{(0,K,\pm1)}$. They act differently on the components of one superfield: $L_{(0,0,2)}$ is trivial on all NS superfields, and $L_{(0,K,\pm1)}$ gives opposite signs on bottom and top components. So they are not lines of the supersymmetric theory. $L_{(0,K,\pm1)}$ reappears below as a global $R$-symmetry.

All numbers come from \texttt{n2\_superfields.py}, checked for $k=3,\dots,13$.

\section{UV: the smallest node containing the perturbation}

The perturbation is the F-term of the chiral superfield $[k,k]\sim[0,-2]$ ($X^k$), plus its conjugate. The smallest node is
\begin{equation}
T_{UV}=\langle[k,k]\rangle=\{[0,2n]\}_{n\in\Z_K}\simeq\Z_K,\qquad F_{UV}=\{L_{[L,0]}\}_{L\ \rm even}=\so,\qquad \mu_{UV}=(\dim\so)^2,
\end{equation}
with $\dim\so=\sum_{L\,\rm even}d_L^2=K/(4\sin^2\frac\pi K)$. In standard labels ($|m|\le l$), $T_{UV}$ contains the superprimaries
\begin{equation}
[0,0],\qquad [k,m],\ m=-k,-k+2,\dots,k,\qquad h=\frac{k(k+2)-m^2}{4K},\quad q=\frac mK .
\end{equation}

\noindent D-terms have $\Delta=2h+2>2$ and are never relevant. So $\phi_{UV}$ is the only $N=2$-preserving relevant perturbation in $T_{UV}$, and it is exclusive to it.

Every nontrivial line of $\so$ generates all of $\so$ (checked). Hence the only nodes containing $T_{UV}$ are $T_{UV}$ ($\mu=\dim^2\so$) and the complete theory $C_k$ ($\mu=1$). $C_k$ contains the F-terms of all $X^j$, and those with $j<k$ are exclusive to it.

\section{IR: the node of the IR operator}

The $(k-2)$ model is approached by the D-term of the non-BPS superfield $[4,\pm2]$ (N2Gab \S5.3, $\Delta=2+10/k$). For $k'=k-2\ge5$:
\begin{equation}
\langle[4,2]\rangle_{k-2}=C_{k-2},\qquad F=\{1\},\qquad \mu_{IR}=1 .
\end{equation}
The reason: $l=4$ generates all of $\mathrm{so}(3)_{k-2}$, and $m=2$ generates $\Z_{K-2}$. For $k\le5$ the field $[4,2]$ does not exist, but any IR superfield with $l\neq0$ and $m\neq0$ again generates $C_{k-2}$.

\section{Selection rules}

\paragraph{(a) $X^k$ alone.} The flow stays in $T_{UV}$, and $\so$ is preserved at all scales (transportability, selection.pdf \S3). The IR nodes available are $C_{k-2}$ only, with $\mu=1\neq\dim^2\so$. So there is no massless IR, as in item (d) of selection.pdf \S3.

This holds even allowing extra gapped vacua. A line cannot connect the $c_{k-2}>0$ vacuum to a gapped one, so it acts on the CFT vacuum by a number $\chi(L)=\sum n_jd_j^{IR}>0$. Positivity forces $\chi=d_L$. But $d_L\in\Q(\zeta_K)$ and $d_j^{IR}\in\Q(\zeta_{8(K-2)})$, whose real intersection is $\Q$ for odd $K$, while $d_2=1+2\cos\frac{2\pi}K\notin\Q$ for $K\ge5$.

The flow is therefore fully massive. It is the Chebyshev deformation, the unique superpotential keeping $\so$ (N2Gab \S4).

\paragraph{(b) The massless flow.} The couplings of $X^k$ and $X^{k-2}$ have dimensions $2/K$ and $4/K=2\cdot\frac2K$, so they are resonant. At $O(\lambda^2)$ the linear deformation $X^{k+2}+\lambda X^k$ therefore also turns on the F-term of $[k-2,k-2]\sim[2,-4]$. This superfield is exclusive to $C_k$, and it is where the second-order obstruction of N2Gab \S3.4 sits. The node becomes
\begin{equation}
C_k\ \longrightarrow\ C_{k-2},\qquad \mu=1=1 .
\end{equation}
The selection rule is satisfied, but it is empty: \emph{no line commuting with $N=2$ survives the massless flow}.

\section{Entropic order parameters and the massive vacua}

\paragraph{Language.} For a pair of algebras $O\subset M$ on a region $R$ with index $\lambda$, the certainty relation (entropic.pdf (3.25)) reads
\begin{equation}
S_{M(R)}(\omega|\omega\circ E)+S_{O(R)'}(\omega|\omega\circ E')=\log\lambda,\qquad 0\le\cdot\le\log\lambda .
\end{equation}
The first term tests the charged operators (intertwiners), the second the twists.

In $d=2$ these two kinds of operator are not distinguished by the topology of their regions. Take a two-interval region with cross-ratio $x$: its complement is again two intervals, with cross-ratio $1-x$. The twists of one are the intertwiners of the other, so the certainty relation just pairs $x$ with $1-x$. For a single interval $R$ (entropic.pdf (3.58)), the complement is two half-lines, and the twist implementing the symmetry on $R$ sits there exactly like an intertwiner of that two-component region.

The phase is read from the large-scale behaviour at fixed geometry (entropic.pdf \S3.4, \S4):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{massless:} conformal functions;
\item \emph{gapped, unbroken:} charged part $\to0$, twists $\to\log\lambda$;
\item \emph{gapped, broken:} charged part $\to\log\lambda_{\rm vac}$ (constant law), with $1\le\lambda_{\rm vac}\le\lambda$.
\end{itemize}
Here $\lambda_{\rm vac}=D^2_{\rm TQFT}$ is the total quantum dimension of the topological (vacuum-degeneracy) operators. For a group it is the number of vacua in one orbit.

\paragraph{Massless flow: lines give nothing.} The node pair is $C_k\to C_{k-2}$ with $\lambda=1$, so every order parameter built from lines commuting with $N=2$ vanishes identically. Nothing topological is forced on top of the $(k-2)$ model by such lines. In particular, no TQFT inside the CFT vacuum is required.

\paragraph{Massless flow: the $R$-parity.} The complete theory has one global symmetry that commutes with the perturbation. It is the $R$-parity $G_R$: $X\to-X$, $W\to-W$, compensated by $\theta\to i\theta$. On bosonic operators it is a $\Z_2$; on fermions it squares to $(-1)^F$.
\begin{itemize}
\item It maps each superfield to itself but acts differently on its components, so it does not commute with $N=2$ and is not a node line. This is the only place where $L_{(0,K,\pm1)}$ enters.
\item Take $O=C^{G_R}$ (the $G_R$-even operators) $\subset C$, with $\lambda=|G_R|=2$. The charged operators are the odd chiral fields, e.g.\ $X$. We use the single-interval order parameter $S_{C(R)}(\omega|\omega\circ E_\psi)$ of entropic.pdf (3.58).
\item \emph{CFT vacuum:} $X\to0$ and $G_R$ is unbroken, so the order parameter $\to0$.
\item \emph{Massive vacua:} $W(v)\neq0$ and $G_R$ flips the sign of $W$, so a massive vacuum is never $G_R$-invariant: $\lambda_{\rm vac}\ge2$. The certainty bound gives $\lambda_{\rm vac}\le|G_R|=2$. Hence
\begin{equation}
D^2_{\rm TQFT}=2:\qquad \langle X\rangle=\pm i\sqrt\lambda,\qquad S_{C(R)}(\omega|\omega\circ E_\psi)\to\log2 ,
\end{equation}
with a constant law, while the twist parameter $\to0$.
\end{itemize}
This uses only the certainty relation and $W(v)\neq0$. It agrees with the Witten index, $k+1=(k-1)+2$. The bound covers vacua related by symmetry; vacua not related by any symmetry cannot be bounded this way, and the Witten index excludes them here.

\paragraph{Check with the Chebyshev flow (a).} Here $\lambda=\dim\so\cdot|G_R|$.
\begin{itemize}
\item In a gapped phase a line maps a vacuum to a superposition of vacua with non-negative integer multiplicities, and the largest eigenvalue of that matrix is $d_L$. Since $d_L\notin\Z$, $\so$ cannot be unbroken: it needs at least $\deg\Q(d_2)=\varphi(K)/2$ vacua, and $(k+1)/2$ for the regular action, which gives $\lambda_{\rm vac}=\dim\so$.
\item $G_R$ doubles the vacua.
\end{itemize}
The total is $\tfrac{k+1}2\cdot2=k+1$, exactly the Chebyshev vacua. The same bound shows that two massive vacua cannot host $\so$ for $k\ge5$. This is another way to see that the massless flow must break $\so$ explicitly.

\paragraph{Relation to massless.pdf.} In the massless flow massless and massive physics coexist, in different vacua. The symmetry predicted by the conjecture is $G_R$. The massive operators are the $G_R$-odd ones (e.g.\ $X$, with $\langle X\rangle\ne0$ in the massive vacua). In the CFT vacuum the massless algebra $A_m$ is all of $C_{k-2}$.

\section*{Summary}
\begin{enumerate}
\item With superfields as units, the smallest algebra containing $(0,-2,0)$ is $\{[0,2n]\}\simeq\Z_K$: the superprimaries $[0,0]$ and $[k,m]$ ($m$ odd). Its only $N=2$-preserving relevant perturbation is the F-term of $X^k$. $F=\so$ and $\mu=\dim^2\so$. It has no massless IR, so it gives the Chebyshev flow with $k+1$ vacua.
\item The massless flow needs the resonant F-term of $X^{k-2}$, which forces the complete node: $C_k\to C_{k-2}$, $\mu=1$. No line commuting with $N=2$ survives.
\item The TQFT on top of the $(k-2)$ model consists of two vacua exchanged by $R$-parity. The entropic bound $\lambda_{\rm vac}\le|G_R|=2$ and $W(v)\ne0$ fix $D^2_{\rm TQFT}=2$. There is no topological sector inside the CFT vacuum.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Caveats.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Odd $k$ only; for even $k$ the line $[k,0]$ is invertible and survives (N2Gab App.~D).
\item ``Flow inside $T_{UV}$ = Chebyshev'' uses N2Gab \S4.
\item Index theory for fermionic nets is used at the level of the NS superfield fusion ring.
\item The field argument assumes IR line dimensions lie in the cyclotomic field of the $(k-2)$ model.
\end{itemize}
